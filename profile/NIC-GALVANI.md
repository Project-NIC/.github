# NIC-Galvani — the universal port-module family

**Luigi Galvani — galvanic isolation carries his name**, and isolation is this
family's whole trade. Galvani is not one board: it is a **socket contract plus six
plug-in modules** that carry the network's entire transport layer.

Every unit's input port is the same socket; every Bifrost output port takes the same
modules the other way round. Three transports — **N** (non-isolated copper), **I**
(isolated copper + the ~48 V remote feed — the ONE place the network makes a
voltage), **O** (optical fibre) — times two ends (1 = output, 2 = input) = six
boards, and the whole question "how does this cable enter the station?" is answered
by which module you seat. On the mast everything runs non-isolated with basic
protection; beyond it you pick by **exposure, not distance**.

The module is the **sacrificial part**: the line cable lands on it, the surge ladder
lives on it, and lightning eats a few-crown plug-in while the board lives. Each one
also watches its own feed — voltage and current streamed, an hourly load-step as a
live ohmmeter of the run: cable aging announces itself years ahead.
